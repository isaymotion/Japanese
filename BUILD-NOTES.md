- Trainer words (0.12.0): new words are added at the end of each list, because a word's position is its page address. The app builds every form of a trainer word by rule (including 〜ましょう "let's" and 〜て ください "please do"), shows them in drills and records them. For that reason 殺す (to kill), 自殺する (to die by suicide), 暴れる (to become violent) and 飲酒する (to drink alcohol) were written by the drafting agent and then taken out; they stay in the dictionary and in the interview phrases, where they appear only in safe sentences.
- Writing sheet (0.12.0): the sheet is always printed in light colours; a sheet holds at most 100 characters; at Medium size with two rows of squares, four characters fit on an A4 page.
- Menu language (0.13.0): only the app's own labels are in Japanese (menu, bottom tabs, footer, theme and text size, backup messages). Lessons, tools and page headings stay in English, because the learner is a beginner and the explanations must stay readable. Each Japanese menu item shows its English meaning underneath, and the labels have furigana but no romaji line. The choice is saved with the other settings and is part of the backup.
- Trainer words (0.13.0): the writers were told to leave out any verb whose "let's" or "please" form would be harmful or absurd, so no verb had to be removed afterwards and no form had to be hidden. Words about symptoms such as bleeding, fever or relapse were also left out for that reason.
- Fifth discharge case (0.13.0): there is still no opioid case in this app. The Hablemos opioid discharge case was re-set as Ayaka Nakamura with over-the-counter cough and cold medicine: no product, brand or ingredient names, no doses, no method detail. Where the Spanish case used naloxone and buprenorphine, this one uses locked household medicines, emergency teaching for patient and mother (call 119, turn on the side, stay, show the paramedics the box), no discharge medicines and an outpatient appointment in three days.
- Japanese headings (0.14.0): with the Japanese menu on, headings and tile titles are shown in Japanese with the English underneath, the same pattern as the menu. The one-line descriptions on tiles and all explanations stay in English, because they are what a beginner reads to know what a page is for.
- Stroke-order extras (0.14.0): the diagram opens only when asked for (a button, or a tap on a kana), so drills and flashcards stay quick and the answer is not given away before the card is turned.
- Trainer words (0.14.0): the clinical batch was cut by its reviewer from 30 to 14. Removed: staff-side procedures (to operate, to anaesthetise, to give a drug, to extract a tooth), bodily functions whose "let's" and "please" forms are absurd, and two verbs whose forms could read as provoking or coercive. 断酒する (to give up alcohol) was added for addiction histories.
- Ayaka's admission case (0.14.0): set on the evening of the admission that her discharge case looks back on six days later (a Saturday). Both overdoses are unintentional and she denies suicidal thoughts, plan and intent, but the safety questions are all asked and the teaching point is to ask directly every time. The frightening past event is never described; when a question comes near it she says she is not ready, and the case tells the learner not to press.

## How Japanese is written in the data
- Reading after each kanji run in 《 》: `食《た》べます`. Words separated by one space, particles on their own. No romaji typed; the app generates it.
- Kanji files: one line per kanji, `字 | meanings | on | kun | strokes | group | word=gloss; … | note`.
- Dictionary files: `word | english | part of speech | category | level | note`.
- Menu labels: `data/strings.js`, block `tl`, same notation.
- Rules: `tools/AUTHORING.md`. Check: `node tools/check-ja.js` (must be 0 errors).
